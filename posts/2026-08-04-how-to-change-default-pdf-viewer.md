iPhone doesn't let you set a default PDF viewer through Settings — but with a few simple tricks built into iOS, you can control exactly which app opens your PDFs every single time.

## Why You Can't "Change Default PDF Viewer" on iPhone the Normal Way

If you're coming from Android, this is genuinely frustrating. On Android, you tap a PDF, choose an app, hit "Always," and you're done. iOS doesn't work that way.

Apple has gradually opened up default app settings — you can now set a default browser, email client, and even a calling app. But **document viewers are still not part of that system**, even as of iOS 17 and iOS 18. There is no toggle in Settings that reads "Default PDF Viewer."

Instead, Apple routes PDFs through its own apps by default:

- **Safari** previews PDFs inline in the browser tab
- **Files app** opens PDFs in its built-in viewer
- **Mail** displays PDF attachments without leaving the app

None of these ask which app you'd prefer. They just open the file themselves.

The good news: iOS has a powerful workaround built right in, and once you know how it works, you'll have full control over which app handles your PDFs.

## How to Open a PDF in Any App Using the Share Sheet

The iOS Share Sheet is the grid of apps and actions that appears when you tap the share icon — the box with an upward arrow. It's your main tool for routing PDFs to any app you want.

### Opening a PDF from Safari

1. Tap a PDF link in Safari. It will preview inside the browser.
2. Tap the **Share button** at the bottom of the screen (the square with an arrow).
3. In the horizontal app row at the top, scroll right to find your preferred PDF viewer.
4. Tap it. The file opens directly in that app.

If you don't see your viewer in the row, scroll all the way to the end and tap **More** or the three-dot icon to manage which apps appear in your Share Sheet.

### Opening a PDF from the Files App

1. Open the **Files** app and navigate to your PDF.
2. **Long-press** the file — a context menu appears.
3. Tap **Share**, then select your PDF viewer from the Share Sheet.

You can also tap the file once to preview it, then use the Share button in the top-right corner of the preview screen.

### Opening a PDF from Mail

1. Tap a PDF attachment in a message to preview it.
2. Tap the **Share icon** in the top-right corner.
3. Choose your preferred app from the Share Sheet.

This works for attachments sent through iCloud Mail, Gmail, Outlook — any email app that supports the iOS Share Sheet.

## How to Make One App Feel Like Your Default (Pin It to the Top)

Since iOS won't let you set a permanent default, the next best thing is making your preferred app appear first in the Share Sheet — so it's always one tap away.

Here's how to pin it:

1. Open any PDF and tap **Share**.
2. In the app icon row, scroll to the far right and tap **More** (or "Edit" on some iOS versions).
3. Find your PDF viewer in the app list and make sure it's toggled **on**.
4. Drag it to the top using the handle on the right side of the row.
5. Tap **Done**.

From now on, that app will be the first icon you see every time you open the Share Sheet with a PDF. It's not a true system default, but in practice it behaves like one.

## Save PDFs Directly Into an App's Library

A lot of PDF and document viewer apps on iPhone let you save files to their internal storage. Once a file lives inside the app, it opens in that app automatically — no Share Sheet required every time.

How to do it:

1. Find your PDF in Safari, Mail, or Files.
2. Tap **Share**.
3. Look for an option like **"Save to [App Name]"** or **"Add to [App Name]"** in the Share Sheet actions list (below the app icon row).
4. Tap it. The PDF is now stored inside that app.

Next time you want to read it, just open the app directly and it's there — no re-routing needed. This is the closest experience to "always open in this app" that iOS currently supports.

## What About iOS 18 — Any Changes?

iOS 18 expanded default app settings in a few areas: you can now choose default apps for navigation (Maps alternatives), calling, and messaging. These are real system-level changes.

But **PDF and document viewers are still not included** in iOS 18's default app system.

If you go to **Settings → [Your App Name]**, you'll usually see permission toggles for notifications, background refresh, or camera access — but no "Set as Default PDF App" option. That feature simply doesn't exist yet.

Apple's trend is toward opening up defaults gradually, so future iOS versions may eventually include document viewers. For now, the Share Sheet and pinning method above is the practical solution.

## Troubleshooting: Your PDF App Isn't Showing in Share Sheet

If your preferred viewer doesn't appear when you tap Share, run through these checks:

**Is the app actually installed?**
Share Sheet only lists apps that are installed and have registered a PDF file handler. Double-check it's on your device.

**Try force-closing and reopening the app**
iOS sometimes needs a fresh app launch before it registers the app properly in the Share Sheet. Swipe up from the home bar, swipe away the app, then reopen it.

**Restart your iPhone**
A full restart clears cached system state and fixes a lot of Share Sheet quirks that minor bugs cause.

**Update to the latest iOS version**
Older iOS builds occasionally have bugs where apps don't register correctly in the Share Sheet. A system update often resolves these silently.

**Reinstall the app**
If nothing else works, delete the app and reinstall it from the App Store. This forces iOS to re-register all of the app's file associations from scratch.

## Wrapping Up

The short answer: iPhone does not support changing a default PDF viewer through Settings. That's an iOS limitation that exists across all current versions. But the Share Sheet gives you practical control — especially once you pin your preferred app to the top of the list.
