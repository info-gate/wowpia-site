How to Choose Exhibition Management Services in 2025

Discover what exhibition management services actually include, which features matter most, and how galleries can streamline every show from intake to closing.

Exhibition management services help gallery owners plan, coordinate, and track every phase of a show — from artwork intake and scheduling to sales reporting and artist payouts. The right platform saves dozens of hours per exhibition cycle and significantly reduces costly errors.

If you're evaluating your options, the sections below break down exactly what these services cover, which features matter depending on your gallery type, and what to look for before committing to a platform.

What Exhibition Management Services Actually Include

Many gallery owners discover too late that running an exhibition covers far more than hanging artwork on white walls. A full-service approach touches every phase of a show — from the first concept discussion to the final artist payment.

Here's what modern exhibition management services typically handle:

  • Artwork intake and inventory — cataloging pieces with condition reports, dimensions, provenance, and high-resolution images
  • Scheduling and venue coordination — booking walls, coordinating lighting adjustments, and blocking installation dates
  • Artist and lender communication — contracts, loan agreements, and approval workflows
  • Marketing and promotion — press release templates, social media calendars, and opening event logistics
  • Sales and transaction tracking — recording sales, issuing receipts, and calculating consignment splits automatically
  • Post-exhibition reporting — attendance figures, revenue summaries, and artwork return records

Whether you're organizing a solo artist pop-up or a multi-week group show, each of these components needs a home. The real question is whether you're managing them across sticky notes and spreadsheets — or through a dedicated platform.

The Hidden Cost of Managing Exhibitions Without Dedicated Services

Before evaluating tools, it's worth acknowledging what poor exhibition management actually costs.

Time lost to admin

Gallery staff commonly spend 6–8 hours per week on exhibition paperwork that could be automated: updating spreadsheets, emailing artists for status confirmations, manually calculating consignment figures. Multiply that over a four-week show cycle and you're looking at a full workweek swallowed by administrative overhead.

Errors in artwork records

Manual inventory tracking introduces real risk. A mislabeled dimensions field or a missing condition report can escalate into a dispute with an artist or a collector. Structured data entry in purpose-built software dramatically reduces that exposure.

Missed sales opportunities

When artwork availability isn't updated in real time, staff sometimes offer pieces that are already on hold — or fail to follow up with interested buyers while the exhibition is still live. Integrated sales tracking closes that gap immediately.

The hidden cost of doing nothing adds up faster than most gallery owners expect.

Key Features to Evaluate in Exhibition Management Services

Not all exhibition management services are built the same. Use this checklist when comparing platforms:

Artwork inventory management

Look for structured fields covering artist name, title, year, medium, dimensions, edition number, price, and current status (available, sold, on hold, returned). Bonus points for condition report attachments and bulk import options.

Multi-exhibition scheduling

If your gallery runs overlapping shows or manages multiple spaces, you need a service that lets you assign artworks to specific exhibitions without confusion. Color-coded calendars and drag-and-drop scheduling make a tangible daily difference.

Artist and collector portals

The best services give artists read-only access to their sales figures and submission status without requiring them to email the gallery for every update. Collectors benefit from digital proposals and price lists rather than manually assembled PDF attachments.

Sales and consignment tracking

Real-time transaction records with automatic consignment calculations (for example, 50/50 or 60/40 splits) eliminate end-of-show accounting headaches. Look for export options that feed cleanly into your bookkeeping software.

Reporting and analytics

Post-show reports should be a one-click export, not a manual compile job. Useful metrics include attendance trends, revenue per exhibition, top-performing artists, and inventory turnover rates.

Mobile accessibility

Installation day is inherently chaotic. Staff need to check inventory, mark pieces as hung, and log condition notes from their phones — not from a desktop back in the office. Mobile-first design isn't a luxury; it's an operational requirement.

How Different Gallery Types Prioritize Exhibition Management

Exhibition management isn't one-size-fits-all. Here's how different gallery models weigh features differently:

Gallery Type Top Priority Features
Commercial gallery Sales tracking, consignment management, collector CRM
Non-profit / public gallery Attendance reporting, grant documentation, loan agreements
Artist-run space Low cost, simple scheduling, artist-friendly portals
Art fair exhibitor Fast inventory import/export, mobile tools, booth layout planning
Online gallery Digital artwork showcasing, e-commerce integration

Understanding your gallery model helps filter out platforms that over-engineer features you'll never use while under-delivering on the ones you need every week.

4 Questions to Ask Before Committing to a Service

1. Can it scale with my growth? If you currently run two shows a year but plan to grow to eight, choose a service that won't require a full migration when you get there.

2. Does it fit my local context? Galleries operating in Korea, for example, need platforms that support Korean-language artist profiles, local payment norms, and domestic consignment structures. A generic Western tool often misses these operational nuances entirely.

3. How steep is the learning curve? Gallery staff aren't developers. If onboarding takes two weeks, you've already lost ground on your current exhibition. Prioritize intuitive UX with a short ramp-up time.

4. What does support actually look like? Live chat during installation week matters far more than a static knowledge base. Ask vendors directly about response times and whether support is available in your language.

What a Well-Managed Exhibition Timeline Looks Like

Here's how exhibition management services slot into a gallery's typical workflow:

8 weeks before opening Import artwork submissions, launch loan agreement workflows, and confirm the artist roster.

4 weeks before opening Finalize the installation schedule, activate the marketing calendar, and set up visitor registration if required.

1 week before opening Complete condition checks, assign wall placements, generate installation maps, and activate live price list visibility.

During the exhibition Log sales and holds in real time, monitor attendance, and push status updates to artists through their portal.

After closing Export consignment reports, process artist payments, update inventory for returned works, and archive the full exhibition record.

A service that supports every phase of this timeline — not just the opening-day buzz — is worth the subscription fee.
